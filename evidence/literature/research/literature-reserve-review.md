## Findings

No arithmetic, final-capacity or reference-copy ownership defect was found in the inspected delta. The independent review identified a real finite-capacity regression: unconditional reservation removed intermediate service hooks, causing OOM where the original succeeded. The engineer reproduced it at K1 and K32 and added a conservative guard. Independent differential executions confirmed original success, unconditional-reserve failure and guarded success at both budgets. The recommendation is the guarded version, with allocation improvement scoped to an idle queue.

## Reproduced debt adversary and guard

Build a retired owning ancestor chain leading to a large backing allocation. Arrange a small living source List and independent living filler allocations so the final destination allocation cannot fit until that dead backing is reclaimed. Use identical source preparation, allocation sizes and heap capacity for before/after snapshots, starting append with declared pending work and bytes. At K1, source lengths requiring several geometric steps create the clearest difference in available allocator service hooks. Record actual service units, pending bytes, pool occupancy, return status and diagnostics. Include an explicit pre-poll control to isolate schedule from total capacity and a source alias retained after append to guard copying safety.

The engineer's `tests/memory-research-list-debt-pressure.c` fills a 64 KiB pool, creates a dedicated output-header hole and leaves one retired record in the upper buddy of the destination region. At K1 the record has two fields and the destination needs 2048 bytes; at K32 it has 64 fields and the destination needs 8192 bytes. Intermediate growth occupies the lower free buddy and pays enough service to reclaim the retired record before the final growth. Unconditional reservation fails before that service can happen.

Independent evidence: `build/run-bddgj2v4/literature-reserve-debt-review.json`. Six tiny O2 executions compiled the same fixture against original, unconditional and guarded runtime snapshots at K1 and K32. Original and guarded versions returned zero, verified every copied element and recovered the entire pool; unconditional versions returned one with the bounded-heap exhaustion diagnostic. Compile commands and fixture/runtime hashes are retained. These are independent executions, not replayed claimed results.

The guard reads `rc_pending_count` after output-header allocation. That allocation already services pending work. The count includes active object tasks, detached frames and temporary chunks. Nonzero count chooses the exact entire original grow loop; the branch is not reconsidered mid-growth. Zero count chooses one reservation: capacity calculation has no allocation/callback effects, the runtime is single-mutator, and no user finalizer can introduce concurrent debt. Every omitted service hook would therefore do zero cleanup work. Empty service hooks do not flush frame caches. This reasoning covers system/fixed/lazy incremental profiles; the compiler arena and eager profiles retain direct reservation because that deferred service condition is absent.

The guard restores this allocation-pressure behavior without an unbounded emergency drain or a new scheduler policy. It still needs the engineer's normal post-edit regression checks. Lazy virtual mapping/OS failure is outside the structural work guarantee. Allocation-count claims must explicitly say no pending debt, and earlier unconditional benchmark results must be revalidated or identified by their snapshot.
